Impact Drill vs Rotary Hammer: Which One Should You Buy?

If you're planning to drill into concrete, brick or stone, you've probably come across two popular machines — the Impact Drill and the Rotary Hammer. Although they may look similar, they are designed for very different applications.

Choosing the correct tool improves productivity, reduces fatigue and ensures cleaner, faster drilling. This guide explains the differences so you can confidently select the best machine for your home, workshop or construction site.

What is an Impact Drill?

An Impact Drill, also known as a Hammer Drill, combines normal rotary drilling with a mechanical hammering action. Two internal gear plates rapidly strike each other, creating small impacts while the drill rotates.

This makes it suitable for drilling into:

  • Brick Walls
  • Wood
  • Steel
  • Aluminium
  • Plastic
  • Light Concrete
  • Furniture Installation

Because it accepts standard drill bits, an impact drill is considered one of the most versatile tools for homeowners and maintenance professionals.

What is a Rotary Hammer?

A Rotary Hammer is a heavy-duty drilling machine that uses a pneumatic piston system instead of simple mechanical gears. The piston produces powerful impacts with minimal effort from the operator.

It is designed specifically for:

  • Concrete Drilling
  • Stone
  • Granite
  • Masonry
  • Anchor Installation
  • Industrial Construction
  • Tile Removal
  • Concrete Chiselling

Most rotary hammers use SDS Plus or SDS Max drill bits that transfer impact energy far more efficiently than standard drill chucks.

How Do They Work?

Impact Drill

The hammer action is created by two toothed discs sliding over each other while rotating.

This provides thousands of small impacts per minute but requires the user to apply pressure while drilling.

Rotary Hammer

A compressed air piston strikes the drill mechanism internally, producing much stronger impact energy.

The machine performs most of the work, allowing faster drilling with significantly less effort.

Advantages of an Impact Drill

✔ Lightweight

Easy to carry and comfortable for prolonged household use.

✔ Multi-Purpose

Suitable for drilling wood, metal, plastic and light masonry using standard drill bits.

✔ Lower Cost

Generally more affordable than rotary hammers, making it ideal for DIY users.

✔ Compact Size

Easy to store and transport between job sites.

Advantages of a Rotary Hammer

✔ Maximum Drilling Power

Designed specifically for reinforced concrete, stone and masonry applications with minimal effort.

✔ Faster Drilling

Completes concrete drilling much faster than an impact drill, saving valuable working time.

✔ Chiselling Function

Most rotary hammers support chiselling for removing tiles, plaster and concrete.

✔ Less User Fatigue

The pneumatic hammer mechanism performs most of the work, reducing strain during long drilling sessions.

Limitations

Impact Drill
  • Slower in reinforced concrete
  • Requires more physical pressure
  • Not suitable for demolition work
  • Produces more vibration while drilling hard surfaces
Rotary Hammer
  • Higher purchase cost
  • Heavier than an impact drill
  • Requires SDS drill bits
  • Less practical for everyday household work
